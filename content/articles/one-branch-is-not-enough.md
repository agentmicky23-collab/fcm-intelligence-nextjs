The days of just operating one premises are gone. You need more than one stream of income, because it is genuinely tough to make any sort of profit in this environment.

## What works this year may not work next year

Being in business means trying to predict what's coming. What will the government do with National Insurance and the minimum wage? What will utilities and oil prices do? None of it stands still, so what works for you this year may not work for you next year.

Last year we ran seven petrol station forecourts. When the numbers stopped working on five of them, we let them go. Today we run two.

## The cost of people

Labour is the biggest pressure. With wages where they are, it is harder than ever to employ people in management and key responsible roles.

When you are small, like I used to be, you wear every hat yourself. You're the postmaster, the manager, the bookkeeper and often the one on the counter. Once you pass a certain level of growth you can start employing people into those roles, but that step now costs so much that it is difficult to sustain if you don't know what you're doing.

## Growth doesn't only go up

Over 15 years I have run up to 100 Post Office branches. Today we run around 43, alongside two Banking Hubs, two forecourts and a food franchise.

Those numbers move because every year we let go of the sites that aren't profitable, rebuild, and keep the ones that are performing. Holding on to a branch that loses money because you're attached to it is how good operators get into trouble.

We have another ten Post Offices in the pipeline, and part of my job is to keep looking for franchises, partnerships and other areas of growth for the business.

## Why I now help other operators

My team is built to handle up to 100 branches. We run 43, so it has room.

Instead of only buying branches myself, I now help other operators buy, run and keep hold of what they have, so that they can grow too, at a fraction of the cost of employing their own management:

- Ways to improve your sales
- Your accounts, business plan and day-to-day operations reviewed, to show exactly where you're losing money
- Interim management if you need a break: my team runs your branch day to day for a minimum of six months, as we already do for Post Office
