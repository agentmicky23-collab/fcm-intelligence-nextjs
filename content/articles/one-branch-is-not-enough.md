The days of just operating one premises are gone. You need more than one stream of income, because it is genuinely tough to make any sort of profit in this environment.

## The cost of people

Labour is the biggest pressure. With the minimum wage where it is, it is harder than ever to employ people in management and key responsible roles.

When you are small, like I used to be, you wear every hat yourself. You're the postmaster, the manager, the bookkeeper and often the one on the counter. Once you pass a certain level of growth you can start employing people into those roles, but that step now costs so much that it is difficult to sustain if you don't know what you're doing.

## Growth doesn't only go up

Over 15 years I have run up to 100 Post Office branches. Today we run around 43, alongside two Banking Hubs, petrol station forecourts and a food franchise. We have run up to seven forecourts.

Those numbers move because every year we let go of the sites that aren't profitable, rebuild, and keep the ones that are performing. That is normal. Holding on to a branch that loses money because you're attached to it is how good operators get into trouble.

We have another ten Post Offices in the pipeline, and part of my job is to keep looking for franchises, partnerships and any other areas of growth for the business.

## Why I share my team

I've already built the team, and it is built to handle up to 100 branches. We run 43. That spare capacity is the point.

Instead of only buying branches myself, I now help other operators buy, manage and maintain what they have, so that they can grow too. You get the people and the systems that run my estate (HR, bookkeeping and regional managers) at a fraction of the cost of employing your own management.
