Everyone talks about Post Office fees. Hardly anyone talks about the lease, and it's one of the biggest decisions you'll make.

## Why leasehold looks attractive

A leasehold branch is almost always cheaper to buy. For a first-time buyer with a limited budget, that's tempting, and in the first few years the numbers often look fine.

Then the rent reviews start. I regularly see increases of 7% or more a year. On their own, maybe you can absorb them. But they don't come on their own. Staff costs go up, business rates go up, utilities go up, council tax goes up. Your Post Office income doesn't rise to match.

Over five or ten years, a lease can quietly eat the margin you bought the business for.

## Why I lean towards freehold

With a freehold there's no landlord and no rent review. You're not exposed to someone else's decisions about your premises. And if you pick the right location, the property itself can be worth a lot more than you paid for it.

For a family running the branch themselves and working behind the counter, freehold is almost always the right move in my view.

## When leasehold can work

I'm not saying never buy leasehold. If you already run several branches with strong cash flow, a well-negotiated lease can work. The key is getting the lease where you want it, with the least possible impact on your costs, before you commit. Look hard at the review terms, the length remaining and what happens at the end.

## Value the property separately

One more thing, and it applies to every freehold deal. A business valuer is not a property valuer. Get an independent estate agent to value the building on its own, separate from the business. Don't rely on the broker's figure for both.

> Leasehold looks affordable in year one. Freehold is still yours in year ten.
