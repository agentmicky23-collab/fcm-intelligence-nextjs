When I'm looking at a branch, I ask for five years of company accounts and five years of remuneration statements. Not one, not three. Five.

## Why five?

Because some sellers improve the figures in the run-up to a sale. Costs get deferred, hours get cut, the owner works extra shifts for free. For a year or two the business looks better than it really is.

Five years lets you see the real trend. Put the early years next to the recent ones. If profit suddenly jumps in the last year or two, I want a clear explanation. Sometimes there is one. Often there isn't.

## What I compare

- **Post Office remuneration year on year.** Is it steady, growing or slipping? Which products are driving it?
- **Retail turnover and margin.** Is the shop side pulling its weight?
- **Staff costs as a share of income.** Watch for years where they look suspiciously low.
- **One-off items.** Grants, insurance payouts or asset sales that flatter a single year.

## If the seller won't provide them

If a seller will only give you one or two years, treat that as a red flag. There may be an innocent reason, but it's on them to explain it, not on you to take the risk.

Then do what I do on every deal: contact the broker for the detail, get the accounts and remuneration statements from the owners, run the research on the location, and only then work out an offer from the real numbers.
