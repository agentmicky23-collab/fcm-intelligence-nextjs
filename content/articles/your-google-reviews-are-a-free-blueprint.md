A customer came into one of our branches recently expecting a service we don't offer. They queued, got frustrated, took it out on the staff and left a one-star review.

It happens. When you're serving 3,000 people a week, one or two will always leave unhappy, and they're the ones who pick up their phone. The happy customers almost never leave a review. So if you do nothing, your online reputation ends up written by the few people who had a bad day.

## What we do about it

- **We respond.** Someone reaches out to unhappy customers and tries to put things right.
- **We ask our regulars.** Our best customers are happy to scan a QR code at the counter that takes them straight to Google Reviews.
- **We keep the basics current.** Opening times, photos, and the products and services listed. People google where to go even when they already know the way.

Google and social media drive footfall now. You can't ignore them.

## The mistake I see new operators make

The most expensive mistake is not looking. If you dig into what people say online about a branch, you'll find comments about the staff, the premises and the service. That's a free list of exactly what to fix first.

The second mistake is staff contracts that only pay people to turn up and serve. Build accountability in, with customer service expectations your team is measured against.

## If you're buying

Read every review of the branch you're looking at, especially the recent ones. Patterns in complaints tell you what you're really inheriting, and what it'll take to turn around.
