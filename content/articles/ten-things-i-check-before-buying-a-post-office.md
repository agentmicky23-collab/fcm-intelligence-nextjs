People ask me what I look at first when a Post Office comes up for sale. The honest answer is that I look at the same ten things every time, in roughly the same order, and I don't let a nice shopfront or a keen broker rush me past any of them.

Here's the list.

## 1. When was it last audited?

Ask when Post Office Ltd last audited the branch and whether anything is still outstanding. One clean audit tells you a bit. A history of audits tells you a lot more. If there's a pattern of problems, that pattern usually comes with the branch.

## 2. The remuneration statements

The listing gives you a headline figure. The remuneration statements show you where that money actually comes from. Which products earn the most? Is the income spread across banking, mails, bill payments and travel, or is it leaning on one thing that could disappear?

## 3. Footfall

Is it genuinely a busy area, or does it just look busy on the day you visit? Ask for the real figures. Then walk the street. Which shops are nearby, are they doing well, and what brings people to this spot in the first place?

## 4. Five years of accounts, not three

This one is non-negotiable for me. Some sellers dress up the figures in the two years before they sell. Five years shows you the real trend. If there's a sudden jump in year four or five, I want to know exactly why.

## 5. Staff and TUPE

Under TUPE the staff transfer to you with their contracts, their terms and their length of service. You can't just change things on day one. Find out who's coming with the business, what they're paid, their hours, and whether anyone long-serving would carry a big redundancy cost.

## 6. Untaken holiday

This is the hidden cost that catches people out. You'll be paying for holiday your staff built up under the previous owner. Get the balances in writing before completion and negotiate them into the price.

## 7. Utilities, rates and council tax

Get the current costs for gas, electric, water and broadband. Are they fixed or variable, and when do they renew? Check the business rates too. I always build a 10 to 20% buffer into my projections, because these only go one way.

## 8. Losses and suspensions

Has the branch had big cash losses, suspensions or warnings? What does the transaction correction history look like? Any insurance claims? This affects your relationship with Post Office Ltd from the start, so don't find out afterwards.

## 9. Competition

How many Post Offices are within one, two and three miles? Has one opened recently, or closed? A closure can be an opportunity or a warning, and you need to know which. Look at supermarket counters, parcel shops and banking hubs as well.

## 10. Local reputation and online reputation

These are two different things, and you need to check both. Talk to the businesses either side. Then read the Google reviews, Facebook and anything else you can find. Look for patterns, and give recent reviews more weight than old ones.

## What this list is really for

None of these on their own will make or break a deal. What the list does is stop you falling in love with a branch before you understand it. If a seller can't or won't give you what you need on any of these ten, that tells you something too.
