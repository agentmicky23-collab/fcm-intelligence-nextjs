It is very easy to buy a Post Office. It is very, very tough to sell one.

That's something I wish more buyers heard before they signed.

## Why selling is harder than it used to be

Costs rise every year. Consumer confidence is fragile. High streets are losing shops. A branch that feels secure today can look very different in five years if the businesses around it close.

The worst place to be is the last shop open on an empty high street. You'll still have the costs, but fewer customers, and very few people wanting to buy it from you.

## Ask the five-year question

Before you commit to any branch, ask one question: what will this street look like in five years?

- Are new homes being built nearby, or are people moving away?
- What does the council's local plan say about the area?
- Are the anchor shops, like the supermarket, pharmacy or bank, staying or going?
- Are empty units being filled, or are more appearing?

## Your exit is part of the deal

When you work out what to pay, think about what someone else would pay you for it later. A freehold in a growing area gives you options. A short lease on a declining parade gives you very few.

The entry price gets all the attention. The exit is where you find out whether it was a good deal.
