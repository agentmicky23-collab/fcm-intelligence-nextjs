The asking price is the number everyone focuses on. The costs that hurt new postmasters are the ones that turn up after they've got the keys.

These are the ones I see catch people out most often.

1. **Untaken staff holiday.** You pay for holiday built up under the previous owner. Get balances in writing and negotiate them into the price.
2. **Redundancy liability.** Long-serving staff transfer under TUPE with their full service. If you ever need to restructure, that history comes with a cost.
3. **Utility and rates increases.** Fixed deals end. Budget 10 to 20% more than the current figures.
4. **Stock write-offs and shrinkage.** Old or damaged stock you inherit, and losses you didn't know about.
5. **Transaction corrections.** Errors at the counter come back to you. Good processes and training matter from day one.
6. **Equipment.** Tills, safes, printers and security kit all need maintaining and eventually replacing.
7. **Compliance training.** You and your team need to be trained properly, and that takes time and money.
8. **Uniforms and supplies.** Small on their own, but they add up.
9. **Bank charges and card fees.** Check them against the volume of transactions you expect.
10. **Professional fees.** Accountant, solicitor and payroll, every year, not just at purchase.

None of these should put you off. They should go into your numbers before you make an offer, not after. This is exactly why I tell people to keep 35 to 40% of their budget in reserve.
