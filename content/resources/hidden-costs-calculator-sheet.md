The asking price is the number everyone looks at. The costs that hurt new postmasters are the ones that turn up after they've got the keys. Fill this in for every branch you're serious about, and do it before you make an offer.

## How to use this sheet

- Work through each table and write your own estimate in the last column.
- Where you don't know a figure, get a quote or ask the seller. Don't guess low.
- For anything that renews, such as utilities or insurance, add a 10 to 20% buffer on top of today's figure.
- Add the totals at the end, then check them against your reserve.

## The reserve

This is the part people skip. My rule is simple: never spend 100% of your budget. Keep 35 to 40% of it in reserve.

That money is for the things you can't see coming. Problems during the deal, surprises at takeover, and the first six months while you find your feet. Something will happen. You need a safety net when it does.

| Line | Figure (£) |
|---|---|
| A. Your total budget | |
| B. Reserve at 35% (A × 0.35) | |
| C. Reserve at 40% (A × 0.40) | |
| D. Most you should commit (A minus B or C) | |
| E. Asking price or your offer | |
| F. Total of all subtotals above | |
| G. E plus F | |

The costs on this sheet are the ones you can see coming. The reserve is for the ones you can't. Don't let one eat into the other.

If G is more than D, you're stretching. Either negotiate the price down, or keep looking. For example, on a £200,000 budget, 35 to 40% is £70,000 to £80,000 you don't touch.

The buyers who get into trouble are nearly always the ones who stretched to the limit to get the branch they wanted.
