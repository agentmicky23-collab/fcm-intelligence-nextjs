The first call with the broker is where you find out whether a branch is worth your time. Brokers want a sale, so they lead with the good news. Your job is to get the detail, and to notice what they can't or won't answer.

## Before you call

- Have the listing in front of you, with the asking price written down. Prices change, so confirm it on the call.
- Only call about two or three branches at a time. Focused beats scattered.
- Take notes. Ask them to confirm the important answers by email.

## The business

1. What is the turnover, and how much of it is Post Office and how much is retail?
2. What does the owner take out of the business each year?
3. Can the seller provide five years of accounts?
4. What's included in the price? Fixtures, equipment, stock?
5. Is stock included, or bought separately at valuation on the day?
6. Are there any other income streams, such as a mailwork contract, lottery, or other agencies?

**Good answer:** a clear split between Post Office and retail, and "yes, five years are available."
**Worrying answer:** one combined turnover figure with no breakdown, or "the accountant has those, we'll see."

## The Post Office side

1. What is the annual Post Office remuneration, and can I see the monthly statements?
2. What are the daily session counts?
3. What type of contract is it: Mains, Local or SPSO? If it's Local or SPSO, are the figures from before April 2026, when those contracts were paid less per transaction?
4. When was the last audit, and was anything outstanding?
5. Any losses, suspensions or transaction correction problems?
6. Is Post Office planning any changes to this branch or others nearby?
7. Does the branch get any support payments or temporary top-ups? On a Mains branch, can I see the figures without the 4% top-up that ends in April 2027? Is the Major Branch Support Payment or Remote Support Payment being claimed?

**Good answer:** statements and session counts sent within a few days.
**Worrying answer:** "the income is about £X" with nothing to back it up, or no session data at all. I treat a seller who won't share session data as a reason to walk away.

## Staff

1. How many staff, on what hours, and how long has each been there?
2. Are they on written contracts?
3. How much holiday is currently owed to each person?
4. Does the owner or their family work in the branch? Will they stay?
5. Do the staff know the business is for sale?
6. Any disciplinary issues, grievances or claims in the last couple of years?

**Good answer:** a staff list with start dates, hours and pay, and a stable team.
**Worrying answer:** the owner works 60 unpaid hours a week that aren't in the figures. That's a cost you'll have to pay for.

## Property and lease

**If it's leasehold:**

1. How long is left on the lease?
2. What's the rent, and when is the next review? How are reviews calculated?
3. Who is responsible for repairs?
4. Is there a break clause, and who holds it?
5. Will the landlord consent to the lease being assigned?

**If it's freehold:**

1. How was the property value worked out, and by whom?
2. Is there accommodation, and what condition is it in?
3. Any known building issues, such as roof, damp or electrics?

**Good answer:** a long lease with clear review terms, or a freehold with the building valued separately from the business.
**Worrying answer:** "the rent reviews are standard." Get the actual terms. I regularly see increases of 7% or more a year.

## The seller

1. Why are they selling?
2. How long have they owned it?
3. How long has it been on the market?
4. Has the price changed since it was listed?
5. Will they help with the handover, and for how long?

**Good answer:** a clear reason, such as retirement, and a willing handover.
**Worrying answer:** a vague reason, a long time on the market, or several price drops with no explanation.

## The sale

1. Have there been other offers? Did any fall through, and why?
2. What timescale is the seller working to?
3. Is it a sale of the business and assets, or of the company shares? It changes which liabilities you take on, so ask your solicitor.
4. What does the seller need from me to move forward?
5. Who else needs to approve the sale? Post Office has to approve you as the new postmaster, through your business plan and an interview, before you're given a handover date. Does the timescale allow for that?

**Good answer:** a realistic timescale and a straight answer on past offers.
**Worrying answer:** pressure to decide this week, or a buyer who "pulled out" with no reason given.

## After the call

- Write down everything you were told, and what you weren't.
- Chase anything promised within a week. How fast the paperwork arrives tells you a lot.
- Only then decide whether to view.
