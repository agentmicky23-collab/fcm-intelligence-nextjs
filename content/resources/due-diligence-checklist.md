I run the same ten checks on every Post Office I look at, in roughly the same order. I don't let a nice shopfront or a keen broker rush me past any of them. Work through this list before you spend money on solicitors, and tick things off as you get them.

## How I use this list

The order matters less than finishing it. My process on every deal is:

1. Contact the broker and get everything they have.
2. Talk to the owners. Ask for five years of company accounts and five years of remuneration statements.
3. Research the location properly.
4. Put all the numbers together in one place.
5. Work out an offer from the real numbers, not the asking price.
6. View the business and the property separately.

If a seller can't or won't give you something on this list, write that down too. It tells you something.

## 1. Audit history

**Why it matters.** When Post Office last audited the branch, and what they found, tells you how the branch has been run. A pattern of problems usually comes with the branch.

- [ ] Date of the last Post Office audit
- [ ] Audit outcomes for as many years as the seller has them
- [ ] Anything still open or outstanding from any audit
- [ ] Any follow-up actions Post Office asked for, and whether they were done

**Red flags:** the seller is vague about dates, outstanding actions, or "we had a small issue but it's sorted" with nothing in writing.

## 2. Remuneration statements

**Why it matters.** The listing gives you one headline number. The statements show where the money actually comes from.

- [ ] Monthly remuneration statements for the last five years
- [ ] Income split by product: banking, mails, bill payments, travel, other
- [ ] Contract type confirmed: Mains, Local or SPSO. Since April 2026 all three are paid the same rate per transaction, so Local and SPSO figures from before then understate what the branch earns now
- [ ] Mains branches: figures with and without the temporary 4% top-up, which runs from April 2026 trading to April 2027
- [ ] Major Branch Support Payment (town and city centres with two or more working banks within a quarter of a mile): is it being claimed, and will the branch still qualify?
- [ ] Remote Support Payment (fewer than 1,500 residents within half a mile, £5,000 a year): is it being claimed if the branch qualifies?
- [ ] Any other fixed payments or trial payments, and whether they are due to end

**Red flags:** income leaning heavily on one product that could move elsewhere. Recent figures lifted by a temporary payment you won't keep. Statements that don't match the listing.

## 3. Footfall and sessions

**Why it matters.** A branch can look busy on the day you visit. Session numbers tell you what actually happens at the counter.

- [ ] Daily session counts, not monthly totals
- [ ] How sessions have moved over the last few years
- [ ] Busiest and quietest days and times
- [ ] Your own count: stand outside at different times on different days

**Red flags:** the seller won't share session data. Sessions falling year after year. Footfall that depends on one neighbour that might close.

## 4. Five years of accounts

**Why it matters.** Some sellers dress up the figures in the two years before they sell. Five years shows the real trend.

- [ ] Five years of full company accounts
- [ ] Turnover split between the Post Office and the retail side
- [ ] Staff costs as a share of income, year by year
- [ ] Any one-off items: grants, insurance payouts, asset sales
- [ ] How many hours the owner and family work, and whether they are paid for them

**Red flags:** a sudden jump in profit in the last year or two with no clear reason. Staff costs that look suspiciously low. Only one or two years available.

## 5. Staff and TUPE

**Why it matters.** Under TUPE, staff move to you with their contracts, pay and length of service. You can't just change things on day one.

- [ ] A list of everyone who transfers: role, pay, hours, start date
- [ ] Copies of every employment contract
- [ ] Any disciplinary issues, grievances or claims
- [ ] Who is long-serving, and what a restructure would cost
- [ ] Pension arrangements for each person [CHECK: current auto-enrolment duties with an accountant]

**Red flags:** no written contracts. Family members on the payroll who won't stay. Staff who don't know the business is being sold when you're close to completion. See our TUPE guide for more.

## 6. Untaken holiday

**Why it matters.** You pay for holiday your staff built up under the previous owner. This is the one that catches people out most.

- [ ] Holiday balance for each member of staff, in writing
- [ ] The date the holiday year runs from
- [ ] Agreement on how the balances are reflected in the price

**Red flags:** "we don't really track it." Large balances across the whole team.

## 7. Utilities, rates and council tax

**Why it matters.** These costs only go one way. I build a 10 to 20% buffer into every projection.

- [ ] Current bills for gas, electricity, water and broadband
- [ ] Whether each deal is fixed or variable, and when it ends
- [ ] Business rates bill and any relief currently applied [CHECK: whether relief transfers to a new owner]
- [ ] Council tax if there is accommodation
- [ ] Broadband speed at the premises

**Red flags:** a cheap fixed deal about to end. Rates relief the seller gets that you won't.

## 8. Losses, suspensions and corrections

**Why it matters.** This shapes your relationship with Post Office from the start.

- [ ] Any cash losses, suspensions or warnings
- [ ] Transaction correction history
- [ ] Insurance claims, including robberies or break-ins
- [ ] Security measures in place and their condition

**Red flags:** repeated losses, a past suspension, or a story that changes when you ask twice.

## 9. Competition

**Why it matters.** A closure nearby can be an opportunity or a warning. You need to know which.

- [ ] Post Office branches within one, two and three miles
- [ ] Any that opened or closed recently
- [ ] Supermarket counters, parcel shops and banking hubs nearby
- [ ] Bank branches that have closed or are due to close

**Red flags:** a new full-service branch opening close by. Parcel-only drop-off points are usually much less of a threat than a full counter.

## 10. Reputation, local and online

**Why it matters.** These are two different things. Patterns in complaints tell you what you're really inheriting.

- [ ] Talk to the businesses either side
- [ ] Read every Google review, giving recent ones more weight
- [ ] Check Facebook and local community groups
- [ ] Note what people say about staff, queues, opening hours and the premises

**Red flags:** repeated complaints about the same member of staff, closing early, or the branch being shut without notice.

## One more: the property

Not one of the ten, but I never skip it. A business valuer is not a property valuer. For a freehold, get an independent estate agent to value the building on its own. For a leasehold, read the rent review terms, the length left and what happens at the end.

- [ ] Independent property valuation (freehold)
- [ ] Full lease, rent review terms and repairing obligations (leasehold)
