It is very easy to buy a Post Office. It is very, very tough to sell one. Before I commit to any branch, I ask one question: what will this street look like in five years? This checklist is how I answer it.

## How to score it

Score each of the ten areas below:

- **2** if it points to a stronger street in five years
- **1** if it's neutral or you can't tell yet
- **0** if it points to a weaker street

Add up the total out of 20. It's a guide, not a formula. One serious problem, such as the main supermarket leaving, can outweigh a good score everywhere else.

## 1. The council's local plan

The local plan shows what the council wants the area to become: new homes, regeneration, town centre changes, or land allocated for something else.

- [ ] Find the local plan on the council's website and read the parts covering your area
- [ ] Look for regeneration schemes or town centre plans
- [ ] Check whether anything nearby is being changed from shops to housing

**Score:** 2 = growth or investment planned nearby. 1 = nothing significant. 0 = the area is being planned down.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 2. Planned developments

New homes mean new customers. Big schemes take years, so check what's actually approved and started.

- [ ] Search the council's planning applications for the streets around the branch
- [ ] Note housing developments, and roughly how many homes
- [ ] Walk the area. Are sites being built, or sitting empty?

**Score:** 2 = new homes being built within walking distance. 1 = some approved, not started. 0 = none, or people moving away.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 3. Bank closures

This can cut both ways. When banks close, people often turn to the Post Office for everyday banking. But losing banks can also mean a quieter high street.

- [ ] Which banks have closed nearby in recent years?
- [ ] Are any due to close?
- [ ] Is there a banking hub nearby or planned, and what does it offer?

**Score:** 2 = banks closed or closing and your branch is the natural place for that banking. 1 = no change. 0 = a new banking service nearby that could take your counter banking.

Check how any change affects remuneration or support payments tied to the location. [CHECK: current Post Office payments linked to nearby banks]

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 4. Shop closures and empty units

The worst place to be is the last shop open on an empty high street.

- [ ] Count the empty units on the street today
- [ ] Ask the neighbours what has closed in the last two or three years
- [ ] Are empty units being filled, or are more appearing?

**Score:** 2 = few empty units, being filled. 1 = some empties, stable. 0 = more closing than opening.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 5. Footfall anchors

What brings people to this spot in the first place? Anchors are the reasons people come.

- [ ] Supermarket or convenience store
- [ ] Pharmacy
- [ ] GP surgery or health centre
- [ ] Schools
- [ ] Station or main bus stops

Then ask of each one: is it staying, growing or going?

**Score:** 2 = several strong anchors, staying. 1 = one or two. 0 = anchors leaving or none at all.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 6. Transport and parking

- [ ] Can customers park close by, and is it free or cheap?
- [ ] Are there parking changes or restrictions planned?
- [ ] How good are the bus routes, and have any been cut?
- [ ] Roadworks or road changes planned that could cut the branch off?

**Score:** 2 = easy to reach, no bad changes planned. 1 = manageable. 0 = hard to reach or getting harder.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 7. Competition

- [ ] Post Office branches within one, two and three miles, using the Post Office branch finder
- [ ] Any that opened or closed recently
- [ ] Supermarket counters and parcel shops nearby

A parcel-only drop-off point is usually much less of a threat than a full-service counter. A nearby closure can be an opportunity or a warning, so find out why it closed.

**Score:** 2 = little full-service competition, or a nearby closure sending customers your way. 1 = stable. 0 = a new full-service branch nearby.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 8. Demographics

Who lives here, and who will live here in five years?

- [ ] Age profile, and who is likely to use counter services
- [ ] Income and spending power
- [ ] Is the population growing or shrinking?
- [ ] Use official census and local statistics for the streets around the branch, not the whole borough

**Score:** 2 = growing population that uses Post Office services. 1 = stable. 0 = shrinking or moving away.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 9. Crime and safety

- [ ] Check the police crime figures for the postcode over the last 12 months
- [ ] Ask neighbours about break-ins and anti-social behaviour
- [ ] Look at the branch's own history of incidents

**Score:** 2 = low and stable. 1 = average. 0 = high or rising.

**Score: ___ / 2**

## 10. Reviews and reputation

Reviews tell you about the branch and the street around it.

- [ ] Read the branch's Google reviews, giving recent ones more weight
- [ ] Read reviews for the neighbouring shops
- [ ] Check local community groups for what people say about the area

**Score:** 2 = positive and improving. 1 = mixed. 0 = poor, with patterns that point to the location itself.

**Score: ___ / 2**

**15 to 20:** the location looks like it's heading the right way. Carry on with full due diligence.
**10 to 14:** mixed. Dig into the low scores and make sure the price reflects the risk.
**Under 10:** think very hard. You'll still have the costs, with fewer customers and fewer people wanting to buy it from you later.

## Why this matters for your exit

When you work out what to pay, think about what someone else would pay you for it later. A freehold in a growing area gives you options. A short lease on a declining parade gives you very few. The entry price gets all the attention. The exit is where you find out whether it was a good deal.
